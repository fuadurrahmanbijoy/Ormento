## B. Blueprint entries

### Ch I. What Economics Is About
Purpose: show why choice is forced on everyone, and what economics studies. Foundation chapter, slowest pace.
Need: nothing.
1.1 The Problem of Wanting More (new idea; C; slow): wants unlimited, resources limited; four kinds of resource (land, labour, capital, entrepreneur); goods and services; scarcity. Household bridge first.
1.2 Choice and Opportunity Cost (new idea; C; slow): every choice gives something up; opportunity cost as the best alternative given up; household example, then the bakery owner. Measured in Ch X.
1.3 What Economics Is (framework; L): the main definitions (wealth, welfare, scarcity and choice) with thinker and date; the central theme; what economics does (explains, predicts, guides choice).
1.4 The Three Basic Problems (framework; C): what to produce, how, for whom; why every economic system faces them; short note on how different systems answer.
New terms: economics, want, scarcity, resource, factors of production, entrepreneur, good, service, choice, opportunity cost, economic system, free good.
Case: Step 1 (2 Feb 2026 decision; see case file).
History (verify): British food rationing from 8 January 1940 to 1954; scarcity answered by "for whom". Sources: Smith 1776; Marshall 1890; Robbins 1932.
X-ref: opportunity cost measured in Ch X; factors return in Ch XV-XVI.
Practice: reviews on scarcity vs shortage, free vs economic goods, three problems; Think it through: a student with Tk 500 and two uses; a family choosing between a bicycle and school books (new data). Ladder (case): familiar household choice; reshaped (a village market stall); new setting (a government with one budget); combined (bakery owner's three choices).

### Ch II. How Economists Think
Purpose: place microeconomics beside macroeconomics, show how economists reason, and where the subject is used in Bangladesh.
Need: Ch I (scarcity, choice).
2.1 Two Ways of Looking (framework; C): micro (single households, firms, markets, prices) and macro (whole economy: total output, price level, jobs); a comparison table; how each depends on the other; definition of microeconomics.
2.2 Scope, Importance and Purposes (framework; C): what microeconomics covers (product pricing, factor pricing, welfare); why it matters to consumers, firms, government; what a student gains.
2.3 Models and Economic Laws (framework; C; slow): model as simplified picture; ceteris paribus; what an economic law is (statement of tendency); its features; how it differs from a natural law.
2.4 Positive and Normative; Science or Art (framework; C): facts vs value judgements; examples; economics as a science, an art, or both.
2.5 Microeconomics in Bangladesh (framework; C): where microeconomic analysis is applied (market prices, farm output, wages, competition rules); prospects; institutions that teach, research and apply it (universities, research institutes, central bank, statistics office, planning body, competition commission). Facts to verify.
New terms: microeconomics, macroeconomics, household, firm, ceteris paribus, economic model, economic law, positive statement, normative statement, welfare.
Case: Step 2 (bakery as a micro unit; flour price rise as a macro event; see case file).
History (verify): Frisch 1933 and the split of the subject; Keynes 1936. Sources: J. N. Keynes 1891; Friedman 1953.
X-ref: models return in Ch IV; 2.5 institutions return in Ch XIII (competition rules).
Practice: sort ten statements as positive or normative; micro or macro; short note on ceteris paribus. Think it through: a rise in bus fares seen from micro and macro seats.
