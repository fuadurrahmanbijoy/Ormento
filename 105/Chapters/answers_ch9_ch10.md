# Short answers for the back of the book: Chapters IX and X

## Chapter IX

1. Business risk comes from the firm's own work and shows in EBIT (for example, a new rival). Financial risk comes from fixed payments to lenders and shows below EBIT (for example, monthly interest).
2. Contribution is Tk 20. Break-even = 1,00,000 ÷ 20 = 5,000 jobs. Break-even sales = 5,000 × Tk 50 = Tk 2,50,000.
3. EBIT at 4,000 jobs = −Tk 20,000; at 5,000 = 0; at 8,000 = Tk 60,000. DOL at 8,000 = 1,60,000 ÷ 60,000 = 2.67.
4. 1,20,000 ÷ 20 = 6,000 jobs. More contribution is needed to cover the larger fixed cost.
5. Each 1 per cent change in sales changes EBIT by 3 per cent. The least value is 1.0, when the firm has no fixed cost.
6. DOL = 3,00,000 ÷ 1,20,000 = 2.5. DFL = 1,20,000 ÷ 90,000 = 1.33. DTL = 2.5 × 1.33 = 3.33.
7. Preferred dividend ÷ (1 − 0.25) = 20,000. DFL = 1,20,000 ÷ (1,20,000 − 30,000 − 20,000) = 1.71. DTL = 3,00,000 ÷ 70,000 = 4.29.
8. 4.29 × 10 = about 42.9 per cent rise.
9. The firm pays no interest and no preferred dividend: it has no fixed financing payments.
10. High fixed cost gives a high DOL. Borrowing adds a DFL, and the two multiply, so a bad year could cause a large loss and failure to pay the lender.

## Chapter X

1. Risk-free return: return with no real chance of loss. Risk premium: extra return asked for extra risk. Required return = risk-free return + risk premium.
2. 5% + 4.5% = 9.5%.
3. Market premium = 13% − 7% = 6%. Beta 0.5: 7 + 3 = 10%. Beta 1.4: 7 + 8.4 = 15.4%. Beta 0: 7%.
4. Required return = 6 + 0.8 × 6 = 10.8%. Risk premium = 4.8%.
5. Reject: 13% is below the required 13.4%.
6. For each 1 per cent move of the market, the share tends to move 1.5 per cent the same way. A fall of 8 per cent in the market suggests a fall of about 12 per cent.
7. Diversification removes most firm-specific risk at little cost, but not market risk, so only market risk earns a premium.
8. Weak: past prices and trading. Semi-strong: all public information as well. Strong: all information, even private. The strong form is broken.
9. They are one idea: the discount rate is the required return on an alternative of equal risk.
10. Projects differ in risk, and investors ask more for more risk. One rate would accept risky projects that deserve rejection and reject safe ones that deserve acceptance.
