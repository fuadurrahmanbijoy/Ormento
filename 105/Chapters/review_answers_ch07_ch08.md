## Chapter VII: Judging Projects

**Think it through.** Coffee machine: payback 2.5 years; NPV Tk 53,589. Juice press: payback about 3.1 years; NPV Tk 38,522. Choose the coffee machine (higher NPV, faster payback). The three-year cut-off rejects the juice press, but its NPV is positive; the cut-off does not change the choice here. The juice press's year-4 inflow would need to be about Tk 1,62,060 (Tk 22,060 more) for its NPV to equal the coffee machine's.

1. Payback: time to recover the outlay. Strength: quick and easy; shows how soon cash returns. Weaknesses: ignores time value; ignores flows after the payback date; cut-off is arbitrary.
2. Running total: 1,00,000; 2,50,000; 4,00,000. Payback = 3.0 years.
3. Factor (8%, 3 years) 2.577097. PV = 50,000 × 2.577097 = Tk 1,28,855. NPV = Tk 8,855. Accept.
4. PV = 27,778 + 42,867 + 55,568 = Tk 1,26,213 (rounded lines), NPV about Tk 6,213. The first project (NPV Tk 8,855) is better.
5. Accept if NPV is above zero, reject if below. At zero the inflows are worth exactly the outlay once waiting is paid for, so the project earns exactly the discount rate.
6. 1,00,000 × (1 + r)³ = 1,33,100 gives r = 10%.
7. PI = 2,70,000 ÷ 2,00,000 = 1.35. Each taka spent returns Tk 1.35 of present value; NPV is Tk 70,000.
8. Choose Y: it adds Tk 70,000 of wealth against Tk 20,000. Payback ignores what follows the payback date.

## Chapter VIII: Risk and Return

**Think it through.** M: EV 10%, variance 21.6, SD 4.65%, CV 0.46. N: EV 15%, variance 160, SD 12.65%, CV 0.84. N is riskier by SD and also by CV (more risk per unit of return). The two answers agree here; they could differ when the expected returns differ much. Share return: (141 + 6 − 150) ÷ 150 = −2%.

1. Risk: the chance that the actual result differs from the hoped-for result. Market risk affects all firms (interest rates rise); firm-specific risk affects one firm (a fire in one shop).
2. Fire at one bakery: firm-specific. Interest rates rise across the country: market. Rival shop opens next door: firm-specific.
3. (235 + 10 − 250) ÷ 250 = −2%: a small loss.
4. Third chance = 1 − 0.30 − 0.45 = 0.25. EV = 1.2 + 4.05 + 4.25 = 9.5%.
5. EV = 8%. Deviations −6, 0, 6; squares 36, 0, 36; weighted 9, 0, 9. Variance 18; SD 4.24%.
6. CV of G = 6 ÷ 15 = 0.40; CV of H = 3 ÷ 9 = 0.33. H has less risk for each unit of return.
7. The expected value shows the centre only. Two investments (or bus routes) can share an average and differ in spread, so a measure of spread is needed.
8. The one with SD 9% is riskier. A cautious owner would ask for a higher expected return to accept the extra risk (Chapter X).
