## Chapter XV. Cost of Capital

**Pause and check (section 1).** (1) 10%, because all the money would come from the uncle. (2) The savings could have earned Tk 1,800 a year in the bank. That reward is given up, so it is a price even though no one is paid.

**Pause and check (section 4).** Total Tk 10,00,000. Weights 0.4 and 0.6. WACC = 0.4 × 8% + 0.6 × 12% = 3.2% + 7.2% = 10.4%. It lies between 8% and 12% because it is an average of the two costs.

**Think it through.** The adviser is correct. Funds are pooled, so the van is tested at the WACC of 11.1%, not at the cost of one loan. At 9% the net present value is about +Tk 4,426 (accept). At 11.1% it is about −Tk 4,653 (reject), because the internal rate of return is about 10%. If the van is riskier than the bakery's usual business, the rate should be higher still, so the rejection is firmer.

**Review questions**

1. The cost of capital is the yearly percentage price a firm pays for its funds. A project is judged by whether its cash, brought to today at this rate, exceeds its outlay, so the rate is needed first.
2. The price the firm pays is the reward the investor receives. The firm calls it a cost; the investor calls it a required return.
3. 14% × (1 − 0.25) = 10.5%.
4. Yearly interest Tk 90. Gain on repayment Tk 40 over 5 years = Tk 8 a year. Average of Tk 1,000 and Tk 960 = Tk 980. Before tax: (90 + 8) ÷ 980 = 10.0%. After tax: 10.0% × 0.75 = 7.5%.
5. Dividend Tk 24. Net proceeds Tk 200 − Tk 10 = Tk 190. Cost = 24 ÷ 190 = 12.6%.
6. (a) 3 ÷ 50 + 0.05 = 0.06 + 0.05 = 11%. (b) Net Tk 47.50. 3 ÷ 47.50 = 0.0632. Cost = 0.0632 + 0.05 = 11.3%.
7. 7% + 0.8 × (13% − 7%) = 7% + 4.8% = 11.8%.
8. Total Tk 12,00,000. Weights: 1/3, 1/6, 1/2. WACC = (1/3 × 8%) + (1/6 × 11%) + (1/2 × 14%) = 2.67% + 1.83% + 7.00% = 11.5%.
9. Present value of inflows = 1,50,000 × 3.6048 = Tk 5,40,720. NPV = Tk 40,720. Accept.
10. Lenders have a fixed claim and are paid first, so they carry less risk than owners, who receive only what is left. Interest also saves tax, while dividends do not. (Any two sound reasons.)
11. Retained earnings are part of the owners' money. The firm could have paid them out, and the owners could have invested them at the same risk. The owners therefore require the same return as on money they put in newly.

## Chapter XVI. Leasing

**Pause and check (section 3).** (1) An operating lease: it is short, may be cancelled, and the lessor keeps the risk of owning. (2) It cannot use the tax saved by depreciation, so the lessor, which can, may pass part of that benefit on through a lower rental.

**Pause and check (section 5).** After-tax rental = 20,000 × 0.75 = Tk 15,000. Present value = 15,000 × 3.2397 = about Tk 48,596.

**Think it through.** After-tax rental = 39,000 × 0.75 = Tk 29,250. Present value = 29,250 × 3.8897 = about Tk 1,13,774. This is Tk 2,176 below the Tk 1,15,950 for buying, so the decision moves to leasing, but only narrowly (the break-even rental is about Tk 39,750). The friend's reason is not sound: a financial lease is a fixed obligation like a loan and adds to financial risk, and many accounting rules now record such leases on the balance sheet. The owner should also weigh flexibility, repair service, uncertainty about the Tk 10,000 residual value, and the reliability of the lessor.

**Review questions**

1. Lease: an agreement letting another party use an asset for regular payments. Lessor: the owner. Lessee: the user. Lease rental: the regular payment. (Example: the leasing company, the bakery, and Tk 42,000 a year.)
2. Operating: short, often cancellable, lessor pays repair and insurance, residual value to lessor. Financial: long, usually not cancellable, lessee pays repair and insurance, like a loan. (Any three points.)
3. Residual value is the worth of the asset at the end of the lease. In most leases the lessee does not receive it, so it is a value given up unless the contract allows purchase.
4. Reasons (any three): little cash at the start; flexibility and avoiding old equipment; service and skill of the lessor; tax benefits the lessee cannot use; fewer loan conditions. Drawbacks (any two): higher total cost; fixed obligations adding to financial risk; loss of residual value; little freedom to change the asset.
5. (1) Find the after-tax cash outflows of the lease. (2) Find the after-tax cash outflows of buying. (3) Find the present value of each at the after-tax cost of debt. (4) Choose the smaller present value.
6. Lease rentals are fixed claims, like loan payments, so they carry the risk of debt. They are discounted at the price of debt after tax, not at the average price of all funds.
7. Lease side: 24,000 × 0.75 = 18,000; 18,000 × 3.2397 = Tk 58,315.

8. 90,000 ÷ (0.75 × 3.8897) = 90,000 ÷ 2.9173 = about Tk 30,850 a year.
9. With no taxable profit there is no tax saving from interest, depreciation or rental, so after-tax and before-tax flows are equal, and the tax benefits of owning are unused. 10. A loan repayment returns money that was borrowed and is not an expense. Interest is the price of borrowing, and depreciation spreads the cost of the asset over its life; both are expenses that reduce taxable profit.
