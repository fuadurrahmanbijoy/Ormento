# Chapter XI answers (for the back of the book)

**Pause and check 1.** The goal is blocked, so relief cannot follow. The worker may feel frustration, then look for another route (ask for a different duty) or become angry or withdrawn.

**Pause and check 2.** The first three levels are met, so esteem is the likely open level. The owner could offer honest praise, a fair title or more responsibility.

**Pause and check 3.** Risk: the group may resist the plan or spread a distorted version. Benefit: it can carry the facts quickly once the owner tells it plainly.

**Review 1.** Motivation is the push that starts and keeps effort toward a goal; for example, a shop assistant who tidies shelves to be seen as reliable.
**Review 2.** Need (wish to be thanked), drive (restless tension), action (extra effort on the delivery), goal (thanks received), relief (tension fades); then a new need.
**Review 3.** Physiological, safety, social, esteem, self-actualisation. Wage: physiological. Safe oven: safety. Lunch table: social. Fair title: esteem. New loaf design: self-actualisation.
**Review 4.** Rows: view of work, source of effort, use of ideas (see the table in 11.1). The manager who checks every sheet holds Theory X; one cost is staff who do the minimum and wait to be told.
**Review 5.** Hygiene factors prevent discontent but do not drive effort (for example, a safe oven); motivators raise effort (for example, recognition).
**Review 6.** An informal group forms by members' choice. Benefits: belonging, quick news. Risks: resistance to change, limits on output.
**Review 7.** Creativity is making the idea of a new pocket; innovation is making, pricing and selling garments with it.
**Review 8.** A team has a joint goal, fitting skills and shared duty; a group need not. A manager can state one clear goal and share the result.
