## A. Blueprint entries

### Ch 1 · Organizations and Managers
**Purpose:** Gives the beginner the vocabulary and the picture of what management is, who does it, and with what.
**Sections:** 1.1 Why people form organisations (everyday bridges: family, team, shop) NEW·Std [foundation] · 1.2 What management is: definition, nature, purpose FW·Land · 1.3 Why management matters; managerial responsibility FW·Core · 1.4 The resources managers work with FW·Std · 1.5 Levels and kinds of managers FW·Core
**Concepts / prerequisites:** goal→organisation→manager (none) · definition, nature (needs 1.1) · purpose, importance, responsibility (needs definition) · resources (needs definition) · levels, kinds (needs organisation)
**Terms:** see bank Ch1. **Case step:** Month 1 (A-1). **Hist.:** Venetian Arsenal as an early managed organisation. **Cross-refs:** functions→Ch2; skills→Ch2. **You will need:** nothing.
**Ladder:** familiar (a household budget run as a team) → reshaped (a school club) → new setting (the bakery) → combined (bakery resources and levels together).
**Practice:** define management in own words; state nature and purpose; name and match the five resources; sort named jobs into levels; Think it through: why does a one-person shop still need management?

### Ch 2 · What Managers Do
**Purpose:** Shows the four-function process, the skills each level needs, and how to judge managerial performance.
**Sections:** 2.1 The management process: planning, organising, leading, controlling FW·Land · 2.2 Managerial skills by level; classic and contemporary views FW·Core · 2.3 Productivity: the ratio TECH·Core · 2.4 Effectiveness and efficiency, kept apart from productivity FW·Core
**Concepts / prerequisites:** four functions, their order and overlap (Ch1 definition) · technical/human/conceptual skills (Ch1 levels) · productivity as output÷input (none; bridge: loaves per hour) · efficiency vs effectiveness (productivity)
**Terms:** bank Ch2. **Case step:** Month 2 (A-2). **Hist.:** Ford's moving assembly line, 1913, and its effect on output per worker. **Cross-refs:** each function→Ch6, 9, 12, 13. **You will need:** definition, resources, levels.
**Ladder:** clean numbers (4 workers, 100 units) → messy (two inputs, changing hours) → descriptive (which function is at work in a story) → combined (productivity plus efficiency plus effectiveness on one case).
**Practice:** match activities to functions; skills needed at three levels with new reasons; compute productivity, then explain the three measures apart on new data; Think it through: can a firm be efficient and ineffective?

### Ch 3 · How Management Thinking Developed

**Sections:** 3.1 Why ideas about management changed NEW·Std · 3.2 Scientific management THEORY·Land · 3.3 Fayol and the fourteen principles THEORY·Land · 3.4 Later approaches: human relations, management science, systems, contingency FW·Core · 3.5 Science, art or profession? FW·Core
**Concepts / prerequisites:** a school of thought (Ch1–2) · one best way, time study, incentives (3.1) · each of Fayol's 14 principles (Std each, grouped) · management science vs scientific management (3.2, 3.4) · tests for science / art / profession (3.4)
**Terms:** bank Ch3. **Case step:** Month 3 (A-3). **Hist.:** Taylor's work at Bethlehem Steel (claims disputed, handle with care); Fayol at the Commentry-Fourchambault company. **Cross-refs:** bureaucracy→Ch9; human relations→Ch11. **You will need:** functions, productivity.
**Ladder:** familiar (a school timetable) → reshaped (the bakery packing bench) → new setting (a hospital ward) → combined (a case that breaks two of Fayol's principles).
**Practice:** state each principle in own words with a fresh example; contrast the two "science" approaches in a table; evolution timeline; Think it through: which principle matters least in a very small firm?

## B. Term bank
*Format: term — plain definition (chapter of first use). ⚑ = "Word in use" note (everyday word, special meaning); max three per chapter. Chosen word = the term shown; use it throughout.*

**Ch1:** organisation ⚑ — a group of people working together for shared goals (1) · goal — a result someone wants to reach (1) · management — getting things done through people and resources to reach goals (1) · manager — a person responsible for the work of others (1) · resource — anything an organisation uses to do its work (1) · top, middle, first-line manager — three levels of managers by distance from daily work (1) · functional manager — runs one specialist area (1) · general manager — runs a whole unit (1) · accountability — being answerable for results (1)
**Ch2:** planning, organising, leading, controlling — the four functions (2) · management process — the continuing cycle of the four functions (2) · technical, human, conceptual skill — skill with tools; with people; with seeing the whole (2) · productivity — output divided by input (2) · efficiency — doing a task with little waste (2) · effectiveness — reaching the goal set (2) · input, output — what goes in; what comes out (2)
**Ch3:** scientific management — studying work to find the best method (3) · time-and-motion study — timing and examining each movement (3) · Fayol's principles — fourteen general rules of management (3) · unity of command — one boss per person (3) · division of work — splitting work by skill (3) · management science — using mathematical models to solve problems (3) · human relations approach — stresses people's feelings and groups (3) · systems approach — sees the organisation as linked parts (3) · contingency approach — "it depends on the situation" (3) · profession — occupation with trained skill, a code and standards (3)
**Ch4:** environment — everything outside and inside that affects the organisation (4) · external environment — forces outside the organisation's control (4) · internal environment — owners, staff, culture inside (4) · stakeholder — anyone affected by the organisation (4) · economic, social, political-legal, technological, competitive, global forces — kinds of outside forces (4) · adapt — change to fit (4) · influence — try to change the outside force (4)
**Ch5:** social responsibility — duty to act for society's good as well as profit (5) · sustainability — meeting today's needs without harming the future (5) · business ethics — standards of right and wrong in business (5) · Davis model — a set of propositions linking social power to responsibility (5) · obstructive, defensive, accommodative, proactive — four response stances (5)
**Ch6:** plan — a chosen route to a goal (6) · strategic, tactical, operational plan — long-range, mid-range, day-to-day (6) · standing plan — used again and again (6) · single-use plan — made for one occasion (6) · policy, procedure, rule — guide to thinking; steps; fixed instruction (6) · budget — plan in numbers (6) · planning premise — assumption about the future a plan rests on (6)
**Ch7:** objective — a specific result to reach by a set time (7) · key result area — a field where objectives are set (7) · management by objectives (MBO) — managers and staff agree objectives and review them (7) · MBO cycle — set, plan, act, review, repeat (7) · measurable — can be counted or checked (7)
**Ch8:** decision — a choice among alternatives (8) · alternative — one possible choice (8) · certainty, risk, uncertainty — outcomes known; odds known; odds unknown (8) · rational decision process — step-by-step logical choosing (8) · probability — chance of an outcome (0 to 1) (8) · expected value — probability × payoff, summed (8) · decision tree — branching diagram of choices and outcomes (8) · decision support system — computer tool that helps decide (8) · bounded rationality — limits on what one mind can weigh (8)
**Ch9:** organising — arranging people and work (9) · division of labour — splitting a job into parts (9) · departmentalisation — grouping jobs into departments (9) · formal organisation — the structure on the chart (9) · informal organisation — friendships and habits outside the chart (9) · line ⚑ — the chain doing core work (9) · staff — advisers who support line (9) · matrix — two lines of command crossed (9) · bureaucratic model — rules, ranks, written records (9) · behavioural model — stresses people, flexibility (9) · coordination — fitting the parts together (9)
**Ch10:** authority — right to give orders and use resources (10) · responsibility — duty to do the task (10) · delegation — passing authority and duty down (10) · span of management — number of people one manager directs (10) · tall, flat structure — many levels; few levels (10) · centralisation, decentralisation — authority held at top; spread out (10) · functional authority — right over a process outside one's own line (10)
**Ch11:** motivation — the push that starts and keeps effort (11) · need — a gap one wants filled (11) · needs-goal model — need → drive → action → goal → relief (11) · hierarchy of needs — five levels of need (Maslow) (11) · Theory X, Theory Y — two sets of assumptions about workers (11) · group, team — people together; people with joint goals (11) · informal group — forms by choice, not by order (11) · creativity — making new ideas (11) · innovation — putting a new idea to use (11)
**Ch12:** leadership — influencing people toward a goal (12) · power ⚑ — ability to make others act (12) · legitimate, reward, coercive, expert, referent power — five bases of power (12) · trait approach — leaders are born with qualities (12) · situational approach — best style depends on situation (12) · leadership continuum — range from boss-centred to team-centred (12) · emotional intelligence — reading and managing feelings, own and others' (12) · transformational leader — inspires change (12) · coaching — develops people one to one (12) · super leadership — leading others to lead themselves (12)
**Ch13:** control ⚑ — checking results against plans and correcting (13) · standard ⚑ — the level to be reached (13) · measure — find actual result (13) · deviation — gap between actual and standard (13) · corrective action — step to close the gap (13) · feedforward, concurrent, feedback control — before, during, after (13) · monitoring — watching without yet acting (13) · controller — person who runs the control system (13) · barrier to control — what stops control working (13)

## C. Case file (illustrative; documented, no narrative; currency Tk, to be confirmed by author)
*Label every use: "Case file, illustrative." Morning Loaf Bakery, one shop, Dhaka assumed. Carry all figures forward.*

**Fixed facts.** Loaf price Tk 70. Ingredients about Tk 24/loaf (flour 0.24 kg × Tk 60 = Tk 14.4 + other). Trading days 26/month. Rent Tk 40,000/month. Start capital Tk 600,000 (owner's savings). Oven 1: Tk 150,000.

| Step | Facts and figures |
|---|---|
| A-1 (M1) | Owner opens as sole proprietor. Staff 4: 2 bakers, 1 counter, 1 rider. Output 200 loaves/day; staff hours 32/day. Resources listed: people, money, materials, equipment, information. Owner = only manager (all three levels in one person). |
| A-2 (M2) | Owner records four functions. Productivity M1: 200 ÷ 32 = 6.25 loaves/hour. New mixer lifts output to 250/day, same 32 hours: 7.8 loaves/hour. Waste 20 loaves/day (10%) → 12 (4.8%). Orders on time 80% → 90%. |
| A-3 (M3) | Packing timed: 40 s → 30 s/loaf after bench re-layout. Two people had been giving orders to the counter; changed to one (unity of command). |
| A-4 (M4) | Flour Tk 60 → Tk 69/kg (+15%); at 60 kg/day extra Tk 540/day = Tk 14,040/month. Competitor opens 300 m away. New labelling rule for packaged goods. |
| A-5 (M5) | Unsold bread donated at close (about 10/day). Compostable bags +Tk 0.80 each, 250/day = Tk 200/day. Labels corrected. Owner sets a written ethics rule: no claim on a label that is not checked. |
| A-6 (M6) | Plan for M7–M12: 300 loaves/day. Premises: flour +5%, demand +8%. Standing plan: hygiene procedure. Single-use plan: festival-week order schedule. Budget set. |
| A-7 (M7) | Objectives: 300 loaves/day by M12; waste ≤ 5%; on-time orders ≥ 95%. Agreed one by one with head-baker candidate, counter, rider (MBO). Review monthly. |
| A-8 (M8) | Decision (risk): A buy oven 2, Tk 180,000: strong demand p 0.6 → +Tk 30,000/month; weak 0.4 → +Tk 8,000. EV = 18,000 + 3,200 = **Tk 21,200**. B extend hours: strong +15,000, weak +9,000; EV = **Tk 12,600**. Payback A: 180,000 ÷ 21,200 ≈ 8.5 months. Chosen after talk with head baker and counter lead: **A**. |
| A-9 (M9) | Staff 4 → 8: production (head baker, 2 bakers, helper), sales (counter lead, 2 counter), delivery (1 rider). Grouped by function. Daily 7 am briefing and order sheet coordinate. Lunch group forms among 4 staff (informal). |
| A-10 (M10) | Head baker given authority to buy ingredients up to Tk 5,000/order; owner stays accountable. Spans: owner 3, head baker 3, counter lead 2. Price setting stays central. Part-time bookkeeper (staff role), Tk 8,000/month, may require receipts from all (functional authority). |
| A-11 (M11) | 2 of 8 staff left in 6 months (25%). Monthly Tk 1,000 team bonus for waste ≤ 5%. Owner moves from checking every tray to agreed targets (Theory Y step). Helper's suggestion: milk bread, 40 loaves/day at Tk 80 (innovation). |
| A-12 (M12) | Owner moves from deciding alone to asking first (consultative) with experienced bakers; stays directive with the new helper. Coaches head baker weekly. |
| A-13 (M13) | Results at M12: 280 loaves/day (standard 300), waste 8% (standard 5%), on time 93% (standard 95%). Deviations: −20, +3 pts, −2 pts. Causes checked; corrective action: oven-load schedule changed; weekly waste count. Controls: feedforward (ingredient check at delivery), concurrent (oven temperature), feedback (monthly profit report). |

**Figures carried forward after Ch13:** staff 8 + 1 part-time; ovens 2; output 280/day; milk bread 40/day; waste 8% → target 5%; owner capital Tk 600,000 + oven Tk 180,000.
