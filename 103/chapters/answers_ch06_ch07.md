**Review questions (short answers)**

1. An account is a named record of one kind of item. A T-account has a title, a left side and a right side.
2. The left side is the debit side; the right side is the credit side.
3. Asset: Cash. Liability: Accounts Payable. Owner's capital: Owner's Capital. Drawings: Owner's Drawings. Revenue: Sales Revenue. Expense: Rent Expense.
4. A debit increases assets, drawings and expenses. A credit increases liabilities, owner's capital and revenue.
5. A debit only names the left side. It decreases liabilities, owner's capital and revenue. Example: paying a supplier debits Accounts Payable, which decreases it.
6. In every transaction, total debits equal total credits. Each rule was built so that a debit is matched by a credit of equal size, so both sides of the equation change by equal amounts.
7. Cash, debit; Accounts Payable, credit; Sales Revenue, credit; Wages Expense, debit.
8. It cannot show that the right accounts or the right amounts were used. A wrong account, or a wrong amount written on both sides, still balances.
9. It lists every account with a number. Numbers group accounts by kind, make them easy to refer to, and leave room for new accounts.

**Review questions (short answers)**

1. The transaction is first recorded in the journal, from its source document. This keeps both sides together, in date order, with an explanation.
2. A source document is paper proof that a transaction happened: an invoice, a receipt, a cheque.
3. Date first; debit accounts at the left with amounts in the debit column; credit accounts indented with amounts in the credit column; Ref. column; then a narration, which says in plain words what happened.
4. A compound entry affects three or more accounts. The total of its debits still equals the total of its credits.
5. Posting is copying amounts from the journal to the ledger accounts. Steps: find the account; write date and amount on the same side; write the journal page in the ledger's Ref. column; write the account number in the journal's Ref. column.
6. The journal's Ref. shows the ledger account number (posting done); the ledger's Ref. shows the journal page. Together they let any amount be traced in both directions.
7. The balance is the difference between the debit total and the credit total. Ending balance = opening balance + increases − decreases.
8. Journal: by date, written first, each transaction once, tells what happened. Ledger: by account, written second by posting, gives balances, tells how much.
9. Chapter 7 teaches the source document, journal and posting (steps 2 to 4). Trial balance: Chapter 8. Adjusting entries and adjusted trial balance: Chapter 9. Statements: Chapters 5 and 12. Closing and post-closing trial balance: Chapter 12.
