## Two people, one set of books

> A partnership begins with an agreement, but accounting must turn that agreement into records.

> Two owners may contribute different amounts and still agree to share a business together.

Imagine that Mitali Bakery has grown beyond what one owner can manage alone. Mitali can supply experience, an established shop, and the existing business. Rafiq Hasan can bring fresh capital and time. They now face a practical question: if two people own one business, whose money is whose, and how is the result divided?

@ partnership | A business relationship in which two or more people agree to carry on a business and share its profits.

> A partnership separates the business from the private dealings of each partner without making the partners into separate shareholders.

Under the Partnership Act, 1932, partnership is the relation between persons who agree to share the profits of a business carried on by all or any of them acting for all.^1 The accounting records therefore need to show both the firm’s business transactions and each partner’s claim on the firm.

> The central accounting problem is not merely recording money received and paid.

The partnership must also record capital contributed, drawings taken, the share of profit or loss, and any amounts the agreement treats as interest or salary. These items affect the partners’ accounts even when they do not behave like ordinary business expenses.

@ partner | A person who is a member of a partnership.

> A partner is both an owner and an individual connected with the business agreement.

That dual position explains why partnership accounting needs separate capital or current accounts for partners. The firm may own the business assets, while the partners hold claims represented by their accounts.

^1: The Partnership Act, 1932, section 4, Bangladesh Laws.

## 18.1 Two people, one business: the nature of partnership

> A partnership combines ownership, agreement, mutual agency, and shared financial responsibility.

> The first feature of partnership is agreement.

A partnership is created by contract rather than by family status alone. The partners agree to carry on a business and establish the terms on which they will operate. The agreement may be written in a formal deed or, subject to applicable law, may be evidenced by their conduct and other facts.

@ partnership deed | A written agreement that states the rights, duties, and financial arrangements of partners.

> The second feature is a common business purpose.

The partners are not merely investors in unrelated activities. They are associated with a business carried on for their common purpose. Their agreement determines how they work together and how financial results are shared.

> The third feature is mutual agency.

A partner may act as an agent of the firm for the purposes of its business. Under Bangladesh law, a partner’s usual business acts can bind the firm within the limits of the law and the partnership agreement.^2 This makes trust and clear authority important accounting and management concerns.

@ mutual agency | The principle that a partner may act for the firm and, within proper authority, bind the partnership.

> Partnership also involves risk.

A partner may be responsible for obligations of the firm according to the governing law and agreement. The important accounting lesson is that the partnership form does not make the owners equivalent to shareholders of a company.

> The advantages of partnership arise from combination.

Two partners can combine money, knowledge, contacts, labour, and judgment. They can divide duties and make decisions together. The weakness is that disagreement can affect the whole business, and the partners may bear responsibilities that a company shareholder normally does not bear personally.

> The accounting records make the relationship visible.

A partnership balance sheet can show business assets and liabilities, while separate capital and current accounts show how the partners’ claims have been built up or reduced.

@ unlimited liability | A form of liability in which a partner may have personal exposure beyond the amount recorded in the partner’s capital account, subject to applicable law.

> The word unlimited should not be treated as a claim that every partner automatically loses every personal asset.

The actual legal position depends on the governing law, the firm’s agreement, and the circumstances. For introductory accounting, the key distinction is that partnership ownership can carry personal responsibility for firm obligations, unlike the usual shareholder position in a company.

^2: The Partnership Act, 1932, sections 18 and 19, Bangladesh Laws.

### What the agreement must settle

> A good partnership agreement removes uncertainty before the first dispute appears.

> A partnership deed usually settles the matters that would otherwise create repeated questions.

It may state the name and nature of the business, capital contributions, profit-sharing ratio, drawings, interest on capital, interest on drawings, partner salaries, admission of new partners, retirement, dispute procedures, and the treatment of changes in the partnership.

> The deed is also an accounting instruction.

If it says profit is shared equally, the appropriation follows that rule. If it gives Mitali 3/5 and Rafiq 2/5, the accounting records must apply that ratio unless the agreement is changed lawfully.

> The law supplies default rules where the agreement is silent.

For example, Bangladesh’s Partnership Act provides default rules concerning remuneration, profit and loss sharing, and interest on capital, subject to the partners’ contract.^3 A student should therefore distinguish between a rule stated by the agreement and a rule supplied by law when no contrary agreement exists.

^3: The Partnership Act, 1932, section 13, Bangladesh Laws.

## 18.2 The partnership deed

> The deed turns a general partnership relationship into a particular financial arrangement.

> A deed should be read as a set of operating instructions.

Suppose a deed states that Mitali and Rafiq contribute Tk 400,000 and Tk 300,000, share profit 3:2, and allow interest on capital at 6% a year. It may also give Mitali a monthly partner salary of Tk 10,000 and charge interest on drawings at 5% a year. Each clause changes what the accounts must show.

> Profit-sharing ratio is different from capital ratio.

Capital tells us how much each partner has invested or has standing in the capital account. The profit-sharing ratio tells us how the partnership result is divided. The two ratios may be identical, but they do not have to be.

@ profit-sharing ratio | The agreed proportion in which partnership profit or loss is divided among partners.

> Interest on capital is an allocation of partnership profit when the agreement provides for it.

It is not normally treated as an operating expense of the firm. Instead, it is part of the appropriation of profit among partners. This distinction prevents the partnership from confusing business operating costs with distributions of the result to owners.

@ interest on capital | An amount credited to a partner for the use of the partner’s capital when the partnership agreement provides for it.

> A partner’s salary has a similar accounting character in the appropriation process.

It rewards a partner for services under the agreement, but in partnership accounting it is generally an allocation of profit rather than an ordinary employee wage expense. The partnership agreement and applicable law determine the actual treatment.

@ partner's salary | An amount allowed to a partner for services under the partnership agreement, normally treated as an appropriation of profit in introductory partnership accounting.

> Interest on drawings works in the opposite direction.

If a partner withdraws money or goods for private use and the agreement charges interest, that interest is credited to the firm and charged to the partner’s account. It reduces the amount ultimately available to that partner under the appropriation.

@ interest on drawings | An amount charged to a partner for drawings when the partnership agreement provides for it.

## 18.3 Capital and current accounts: forming the partnership

> Formation accounting records what each partner brings and separates ownership claims from later movements.

> The first formation question is what assets and liabilities enter the partnership.

If Mitali contributes cash of Tk 400,000, the firm records cash and credits Mitali’s capital account. If Rafiq contributes cash of Tk 300,000, the same logic applies to Rafiq. The entry records the firm’s new resources and the partner’s ownership claim.

> A partner can contribute assets other than cash.

If Rafiq contributes equipment agreed at Tk 180,000, the equipment is debited and Rafiq’s capital is credited. If the partnership also takes over a liability attached to that contribution, the liability is recorded separately and reduces the net capital introduced.

@ partner's capital account | The account that records a partner’s long-term ownership claim arising from capital contributed and other agreed capital changes.

> The capital account can be fixed or fluctuating.

Under a fixed-capital approach, the capital account is kept at an agreed base amount and routine items such as drawings, profit shares, and interest may be placed in a current account. Under a fluctuating approach, those items may be posted directly to capital.

@ current account | A partner’s account used to record routine increases and decreases in the partner’s claim when a separate fixed capital account is maintained.

> The choice is a system choice, not a change in total equity.

If the same amounts are recorded in capital and current accounts rather than one fluctuating capital account, the total claim of the partner remains the important figure. The presentation changes; the underlying economics do not.

> Drawings reduce a partner’s claim on the firm.

If Mitali withdraws Tk 20,000 for personal use, the partnership records the withdrawal against Mitali’s account. The firm has not incurred a business expense merely because the owner took money out.

@ drawings | Cash, goods, or other resources taken by a partner for personal use.

### Formation case

> The first entry is a record of resources received, not a statement of future profit.

> Mitali and Rafiq form a partnership on 1 July 2026 with agreed capitals of Tk 400,000 and Tk 300,000.

The partnership receives Tk 700,000 cash. Their deed states that profit will be shared 3:2. For this introductory case, assume no liabilities are taken over at formation.

> The journal entry is short because the transaction itself is simple.

Debit Cash Tk 700,000. Credit Mitali, Capital Tk 400,000. Credit Rafiq, Capital Tk 300,000. The debit equals the two credits, and the capital accounts now show the agreed ownership contributions.

> The capital ratio is 4:3, while the profit ratio is 3:2.

That difference is allowed because capital contribution and profit-sharing are separate matters. A student who automatically divides profit according to capital has confused two different instructions.

## 18.4 Sharing profit and loss: ratio, interest, salary

> Partnership appropriation distributes the accounting result according to the agreement.

> The income statement first determines the firm’s profit or loss.

Only after that result is known do we apply the partnership agreement to determine the partners’ shares. This is why the appropriation account belongs after the income statement in the teaching sequence.

@ appropriation account | An account used to show how partnership profit or loss is allocated among the partners.

> Consider a partnership profit of Tk 200,000 shared 3:2.

Mitali receives 3/5 of Tk 200,000, or Tk 120,000. Rafiq receives 2/5, or Tk 80,000. The total allocation equals the partnership profit.

> Now add interest on capital and a partner salary.

Suppose Mitali’s capital is Tk 400,000 and Rafiq’s is Tk 300,000. At 6% interest, the allowances are Tk 24,000 and Tk 18,000. Suppose Mitali also receives a salary allowance of Tk 30,000. These amounts are allocated before the remaining profit is divided under the deed.

> The calculation must respect the order of the agreement.

If total profit is Tk 200,000, the allowances total Tk 72,000, leaving Tk 128,000. If the remaining amount is shared 3:2, Mitali receives Tk 76,800 and Rafiq Tk 51,200. Their total appropriation credits become Tk 130,800 and Tk 69,200 respectively.

> The same method works when the result is a loss, but the direction changes.

A loss is not an allowance. It is shared according to the agreed loss-sharing rule, which is often the same ratio used for profit unless the agreement states otherwise.

> A partner’s salary is not deducted twice.

It is first treated as an agreed appropriation. The remaining profit is then divided. A careless calculation may subtract the salary from profit, divide the remainder, and later subtract the salary again from the partner’s final claim. That would count the same allocation twice.

@ appropriation | The process of allocating a partnership’s profit or loss among partners according to the agreement.

 :fig Profit appropriation flow | fig18_2_appropriation.svg

### A complete appropriation

> A complete appropriation separates the firm’s result from the partners’ agreed claims on that result.

> Assume the partnership earns Tk 240,000 and the deed gives Mitali a Tk 30,000 salary, interest on capital of 6% on Tk 400,000, and Rafiq interest on capital of 6% on Tk 300,000.

The allowances are Tk 30,000, Tk 24,000, and Tk 18,000. Total allowances are Tk 72,000. Remaining profit is Tk 168,000.

> If the profit-sharing ratio is 3:2, the remaining profit is divided into Tk 100,800 for Mitali and Tk 67,200 for Rafiq.

Mitali’s total appropriation is therefore Tk 154,800. Rafiq’s total is Tk 85,200. Together they receive Tk 240,000, exactly matching the firm’s profit.

> The calculation is easier when every stage is visible.

First write the profit. Next list each allowance. Then subtract the allowances. Finally apply the residual sharing ratio. A partnership problem becomes much less confusing when the calculation follows the agreement in the same order in which the agreement is read.

## 18.5 The financial statements of a partnership

> Partnership statements show the firm’s business result and then the partners’ changing ownership claims.

> The income statement of a partnership follows the same broad logic used earlier.

Revenue and expenses are brought together to determine net profit or net loss. The partnership does not create profit separately for each partner before determining the firm’s total result.

> The appropriation account then explains how that result is allocated.

Interest on capital, partner salary, interest on drawings, and the residual profit share are shown according to the agreement. The resulting amounts are transferred to the appropriate partner accounts.

> The balance sheet shows the partnership’s assets and liabilities and the partners’ capital interests.

Under a fixed-capital system, the capital section may show fixed capital plus current account balances. Under a fluctuating system, the capital accounts themselves include the accumulated movements.

> The statement remains a statement of the firm, not a collection of personal balance sheets.

Business assets belong to the partnership as the accounting entity. The partners’ accounts explain their claims against the net assets rather than turning each partner into the direct owner of a particular oven, table, or cash note.

@ partners’ capital | The combined ownership claims represented by the partners’ capital accounts.

## Worked case: Mitali and Rafiq

> A partnership case becomes manageable when formation, drawings, profit, and appropriation are kept in separate stages.

> Situation: Mitali and Rafiq begin their partnership on 1 July 2026.

Mitali contributes Tk 400,000 cash and Rafiq contributes Tk 300,000. The deed gives Mitali a salary allowance of Tk 30,000, interest on capital at 6%, and a 3:2 residual profit-sharing ratio. Rafiq receives interest on capital at 6%. The partnership earns Tk 240,000 before appropriation.

> Question: How much of the profit is allocated to each partner?

The question is about appropriation, not about calculating the firm’s operating profit. The profit has already been determined as Tk 240,000.

> Reasoning: calculate each agreed allowance first.

Mitali’s interest is Tk 24,000 and Rafiq’s is Tk 18,000. Mitali’s salary is Tk 30,000. Total allowances are Tk 72,000, leaving Tk 168,000.

> Reasoning: divide the remainder in the agreed ratio.

Mitali receives 3/5 of Tk 168,000, or Tk 100,800. Rafiq receives 2/5, or Tk 67,200.

> Conclusion: Mitali receives Tk 154,800 and Rafiq Tk 85,200 from the appropriation.

The two amounts total Tk 240,000, so the entire partnership profit has been allocated.

> Check: the allocation must not change total partnership profit.

If the partners’ total appropriation does not equal the profit available for appropriation, the calculation contains an error or an unrecorded item.

## Summary

> Partnership accounting records a shared business while preserving each partner’s separate financial claim.

> The partnership begins with agreement, common business activity, and shared profit responsibility.

Its deed establishes the financial rules. Capital accounts record long-term ownership contributions, while current accounts may record routine movements under a fixed-capital system. Profit is first determined for the firm and then appropriated according to the agreement.

> The most important distinction is between business profit and partner allocation.

Interest on capital, partner salary, and residual profit share are parts of the appropriation process in introductory partnership accounting. Drawings reduce a partner’s claim rather than becoming an ordinary business expense.

## Where people go wrong

> Most partnership errors come from confusing capital, profit, and personal drawings.

- Do not use the capital ratio automatically as the profit-sharing ratio.
- Do not treat partner salary as an ordinary operating expense when the problem is asking for partnership appropriation.
- Do not forget that drawings reduce the partner’s claim.
- Do not divide the original profit again after already allocating the residual profit.
- Do not assume the law’s default rule applies when a valid partnership agreement provides another rule.

## In history

> A famous technology company began with a partnership before becoming a corporation.

Hewlett and Packard officially started their partnership in 1939. Hewlett-Packard’s own historical material describes the partnership and its early work in a Palo Alto garage; the company later became incorporated in 1947.^4 The example is useful because it shows that partnership can be an early organisational form rather than a permanent destination.

^4: Hewlett-Packard historical publications and the company’s 2003 annual report describe the partnership founded by William R. Hewlett and David Packard in 1939.
