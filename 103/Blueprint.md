At the guide's density of about 600 words a page this is roughly 360 A4 pages before front and back matter.

**Parts of the book** (for the contents page only; chapters stay numbered continuously):

- *Beginning* (Chapters 1 to 3): business, accounting, the rules behind the records. The slowest chapters.
- *The Method* (Chapters 4 to 8): equation, statements, accounts, journal and ledger, trial balance.
- *The Cycle Completed* (Chapters 9 to 12): adjusting, plant assets, worksheet, closing.
- *Trading Businesses* (Chapters 13 to 16): merchandising, inventory, special journals, bank reconciliation.
- *Other Forms of Organisation* (Chapters 17 to 20): non-trading organisations, partnership, company.

## B. How the order was chosen

**Dependency chain (each arrow means: needs).** Ch 20 → 18, 12 · Ch 19 → 18 · Ch 18 → 12, 13 · Ch 17 → 9, 15 · Ch 16 → 15 · Ch 15 → 7, 13 · Ch 14 → 13 · Ch 13 → 12, 10 · Ch 12 → 11, 10, 9, 5 · Ch 11 → 10, 9, 8 · Ch 10 → 9 · Ch 9 → 8, 7, 3 · Ch 8 → 7 · Ch 7 → 6 · Ch 6 → 4 · Ch 5 → 4 · Ch 4 → 3, 1 · Ch 3 → 2 · Ch 2 → 1.

**Why some ideas sit where they do.**

- The simple statements come early (Chapter 5), straight after the equation, because a beginner can see where profit and the balance sheet come from before meeting debits and credits. Classified statements wait for Chapter 13, when the layout has something to classify.
- The trial balance and the correction of errors share Chapter 8, because the test and the mistakes it can and cannot catch are one idea.
- Depreciation as an adjustment is met in Chapter 9 in one line, then taught in full in Chapter 10, before the worksheet, so that the worksheet is built with a complete set of adjustments.
- The whole accounting cycle is pictured in outline in Chapter 7 and taught in full in Chapter 12, after every step has been learned.
- Inventory follows the merchandising chapter because cost of goods sold must be understood first.
- The partnership is split in two (Chapters 18 and 19), because forming a firm and sharing profit is a different job from admitting and closing partners.

**Foundation material added** (no topic names these, but an absolute beginner needs them first):

- Chapter 1: the vocabulary of buying and selling (goods, service, profit, customer, supplier, debt) and the idea of paying later (*on account*).
- Chapter 2: the household record book as an everyday bridge to business records; the objectives of accounting.
- Chapter 3: why shared rules exist, told as a puzzle of two bakers who report different results.
- Chapter 4: a household's 'owned and owed' as a bridge to assets, liabilities and equity.
- Chapter 6: a notebook with a left page and a right page as a bridge to the T-account; a 'Word in use' note on *account* and on *credit*.
- Chapter 14, section 14.5: a short outline of income tax on profit, needed to compare inventory methods.
- Back of the book: the 'Maths you will use' appendix, the glossary from the term bank, review answers, and 'Pause and check' answers.

**Overlaps settled by one cross-reference, not repetition.**

- Statements: simple form (Ch 5), from adjusted figures (Ch 12), classified (Ch 13); each later chapter points back instead of re-teaching.
- Depreciation: one-line adjustment (Ch 9), methods and disposal (Ch 10); the balance sheet shows it in Chapters 12 and 13.
- Forms of ownership: overview (Ch 1), comparison table (Ch 17), then detail (Chs 18 to 20).
- Equity: owner's equity (Ch 4), partners' capital (Ch 18), shareholders' equity (Ch 20); each pointer names the others.

**Decisions the author is asked to confirm at Checkpoint 1**

1. **Size.** 20 chapters is long for an introductory book. Candidates to merge if a shorter book is wanted: Chapters 2 and 3; Chapters 15 and 16; Chapters 17 and 18; Chapters 18 and 19 (shortening admission).
2. **Digit grouping.** Default in this plan: international grouping (Tk 1,450,000). The lakh and crore style (Tk 14,50,000) is explained once in the 'Maths you will use' appendix. Confirm or reverse.
3. **The running business.** Mitali Bakery, owner Mitali Rahman, Dhaka, starting 1 January 2026, and all figures of Chapters 4 to 12 are proposals (see the State File case file). Confirm names and figures, or supply others.
4. **Ingredients.** In Chapters 4 to 12 the bakery's flour, sugar and packaging are treated as supplies, with the cost of those used shown as an expense. Making goods is treated fully in Chapter 14 (the three kinds of inventory), not earlier.
5. **Inventory methods.** LIFO is taught as one of four methods, with a clear note on whether the standards in force in Bangladesh allow it (to be verified). The tax comparison is a teaching comparison, not legal advice.
6. **Outside this book.** Goodwill and bonus on admission of a partner; preparing a statement of cash flows (it is described, not prepared); dividends beyond a short note. Confirm or add.
7. **Reversing entries** (12.5) are marked advanced and may be skipped on a first reading.
8. **Declining-balance depreciation** is included as a fourth method in 10.5, and specific identification as a fourth inventory method in 14.4.
9. **Law and standards to verify before drafting the chapters that use them:** the company law, partnership law, financial reporting law and income tax law in force in Bangladesh; the status of the international standards there; the rules on a company buying back its own shares; the income year for tax.
